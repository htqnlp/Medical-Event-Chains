01 / The construction method

Time first. Events second.

  1. Extract temporal expressions with a UIE-based method, then split the record into time-associated blocks.
  2. Detect event triggers with BERT + Bi-LSTM, enrich a three-sentence window with Bi-GRU context, and classify triggers with softmax.
  3. Extract arguments using WordTag. Pair each event with its block's time expression.
  4. Order each patient's events; use a shared disease or symptom to combine patient chains and connect to CMeKG.

02 / What the paper reports

Separate evaluations, separate claims.

The trigger detector is evaluated on MLEE: 2,608 samples, with 1,728 for training and 880 for testing. Table I reports P = 83.30, R = 81.90 and F = 81.60. These are the paper's reported values; the F value is not the harmonic mean of the displayed P and R.

For CCKS2020, the paper reports 1,050 EMRs, 4,450 temporal expressions, 1,050 individual chains and 325 combined chains. Its five event categories are Occurrence, Check, Diagnosis, Surgery and Treatment. This example contains four of them; Check is displayed here as Examination.

Table II reports decreasing extraction accuracy as the number of events per time block increases: 93% for one event and 46% for more than six. These figures do not measure the correctness of this hand-curated demonstration.

03 / How to read this example

One concept, multiple patient histories.

This is a deterministic, browser-local reconstruction of the supplied figures, using English translations and condensed source excerpts. Time-expression and trigger highlights have separate presentation stages; trigger detection is previewed on the narrative, although the paper applies its model to time-associated blocks. It does not execute UIE, BERT, Bi-LSTM, Bi-GRU or WordTag.

Arrows express temporal progression within one record. Plain branch links express membership in a shared time block. Dashed links express shared-concept retrieval. Neither event order nor knowledge-graph adjacency establishes causality.

Each “Today” is local to its own record. Relative dates remain relative because admission dates are not supplied. A shared symptom groups complete patient histories without collapsing distinct patients or their events.

Research source

A Time-Guided Method for Constructing Combined Medical Event Chains

Lishuang Li, Tianqi Hu, Xueyang Qin · IEEE BIBM 2023 · pp. 2027–2030

The paper's contribution is a disease- or symptom-centered organization of complete patient event histories. Time expressions establish the structure; event triggers and their arguments supply the clinical content. Chains from different patients connect through a common concept.

Extraction architecture

Temporal expressions: UIE-based extraction
Trigger encoding: BERT → Bi-LSTM
Context: three neighboring sentences → Bi-GRU
Fusion: sentence representation + context representation → softmax
Arguments: WordTag
Representation: [Time, Trigger, Object, Type]

Faithful reconstruction, explicit ambiguities

  • The two illustrated records yield six and five selected events, respectively. The English excerpts retain those events and omit irrelevant clinical detail.
  • The additional single-chain image labels the joint swelling/pain event as a symptom. The combined-chain figure labels that same event as Diagnosis. This demo uses Occurrence and records the discrepancy in its inspector.
  • “Allergic purpura” and the original brand “Solu-Medrol” are retained as translated source terms; drug and diagnosis labels are not additional clinical conclusions.
  • The knowledge-graph neighbors are reconstructed from the supplied figure: cryoglobulinemia, anaphylactic shock, normal saline and dexamethasone. The figure does not name their relation predicates; this demo uses untyped links.
  • The source figure groups rash with the May 2013 endoscopy block; the demo preserves that association without asserting that the examination caused the rash.
